# Who Groundtruth is for

> **Underserved sector: small and mid-sized staffing & recruiting agencies —
> specifically IT and contract staffing.**
>
> They are the highest-risk node in the hiring funnel and the least-tooled
> participant in it. They carry an enterprise's liability on a small business's
> budget.

---

## 1. Why this sector, and why it is underserved

Trust tooling in hiring is built and priced for the wrong end of the market.

Background-check and interview-integrity incumbents sell to enterprises: buyers
with a talent-acquisition operations function, a security team, a procurement
process, and a five-figure annual budget. Their sales cycle assumes a vendor
review. Their pricing assumes a headcount plan.

A staffing agency has none of those things. A typical firm in this segment is
10–200 people, runs on 15–25% gross margin, bills per placement, has no security
team, no procurement function, and no budget line called "trust." Its ATS —
Bullhorn, Ceipal, JobDiva — has no verification layer, and the enterprise
vendors don't call on accounts this size.

So the segment with the **highest fraud exposure in hiring** has the **thinnest
tooling coverage in hiring**. That gap is the market.

### The liability asymmetry that makes this acute

An agency captures a single placement fee and carries the entire reputational
downside of a bad submission.

When a fraudulent candidate reaches a client, the agency does not lose a
placement — it can lose the account. In staffing, [client trust is the asset at
stake](https://staffinghub.com/hiring/candidate-verification-ai-resume-fraud-staffing-firms-2026/);
[23% of affected businesses report losses over $50,000 and 10% over
$100,000](https://staffinghub.com/hiring/candidate-verification-ai-resume-fraud-staffing-firms-2026/),
and those figures understate it, because the real loss is a master services
agreement worth years of revenue. **One bad submission is not a $50k mistake.
It is a $2M ARR mistake.**

The fraud is already here, not forecast: [four in ten staffing buyers report
candidate fraud
today](https://staffinghub.com/hiring/candidate-verification-ai-resume-fraud-staffing-firms-2026/),
with [one in four candidate profiles projected to be fake by
2028](https://staffinghub.com/hiring/candidate-verification-ai-resume-fraud-staffing-firms-2026/).
Proxy-interview marketplaces operate openly on Telegram and Facebook, selling
stand-ins by technical stack, [some with money-back guarantees if the candidate
isn't hired](https://glider.ai/proxy-interview-fraud-detection-guide/).

### The forcing function: agencies are being audited by their own clients

This is what turns pain into budget. Enterprises have stopped taking agency
vetting on faith. The guidance now given to staffing buyers is to write [the
exact identity verification solutions required by the agency into service-level
agreements, and to audit staffing partners
regularly](https://staffinghub.com/hiring/candidate-verification-ai-resume-fraud-staffing-firms-2026/).

That changes the sale completely. The agency is not buying a productivity tool
it might get around to. It is buying **the evidence file it must produce to keep
a contract it already has.** Groundtruth's per-candidate dossier, with its
citations and its PRISM trace ID, *is* that artifact. We sell the audit
response, and the fraud prevention comes free with it.

### The tail risk that gets it bought this quarter

Agencies are the documented conduit for North Korean IT worker infiltration.
Operatives were placed at [at least 64 American
organizations](https://www.axios.com/2025/01/24/north-korea-it-worker-scam-indictiments-doj)
between 2018 and 2024; enforcement in mid-2025 covered [29 laptop farms across
16 states](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/two-us-nationals-sentenced-facilitating-fraudulent-remote-information-technology-worker),
and the FBI is [still working active cases into
2026](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/technology-main/2026/08/fbi-investigating-north-korean-remote-it-staffer-working-for-u-s-agency/).

For an agency this is not an embarrassment, it is **OFAC sanctions exposure**.
That is the rare category of risk an owner-operator will spend money on without
being sold to.

And it is the single cleanest proof of our thesis. **A DPRK operative's resume
is excellent** — well written, plausible, correctly keyword-targeted, often
better than a real candidate's. Every AI-writing detector on the market returns
"human." What breaks the cover is never the prose. It is corroboration: a GitHub
account with four months of history, an employer that resolves to no real
domain, no public footprint predating 2024, a home address shared with three
other contractors.

Detection loses here. Verification wins here. That is the whole company, and
this sector is where the difference is worth the most money.

## 2. The same customer owns the other half of the problem

Staffing agencies are **the most impersonable organisation in hiring**, for a
structural reason nobody can engineer away:

> An unsolicited message from a recruiter the candidate has never heard of,
> about a job at a company the candidate never applied to, is *the legitimate
> business motion of a staffing agency.*

Every consumer-protection heuristic — don't trust unexpected outreach, verify
the sender, only engage with employers you approached — describes normal agency
behaviour. Candidates have no baseline to compare against, so impersonating an
agency recruiter is the lowest-effort, highest-yield identity in the entire job
market.

The cost of that lands on candidates and on the agency's brand at the same time.
Job-scam losses reported to the FTC [rose from $90M in 2020 to $501M in
2024](https://cw33.com/news/local/job-scams-result-in-150-4-million-losses-ftc-reports/);
BBB employment-scam reports [doubled in 2025 to
23,234](https://theworlddata.com/employment-scam-statistics-in-us/); [one in
four 2025 job seekers reported falling victim to a hiring
scam](https://techbullion.com/job-scam-statistics-fbi-ftc-data/). Scammers have
moved to [AI-generated offer letters and deepfake video
interviews](https://techbullion.com/job-scam-statistics-fbi-ftc-data/) — which
means the last human-detectable tells are gone.

So the same engine runs backwards for the same customer: Groundtruth issues the
agency a **verifiable outreach identity**, and gives every candidate a free way
to confirm that a recruiter, a posting, or an offer genuinely came from that
agency.

**One customer. One verification engine. Both directions of the funnel.** That
is why staffing is a wedge rather than merely a segment — and it is how the
genuinely underserved population here, the candidates being defrauded, gets
protected without needing a consumer business model to pay for it. We reach them
through the customer who already has a reason to pay.

## 3. Target customers

### Economic buyer — Agency owner / VP of Delivery
10–200 person firm. Wakes up worried about client retention and MSA renewals,
not about screening throughput. Buys in days, not quarters, and will decide
alone. **What we sell them:** "You can prove every candidate you submitted was
verified, on the day you submitted them."

### User — Recruiter / bench sales rep
Carries 20–40 open requisitions simultaneously. Paid on placements. Will abandon
any tool that adds a step. **What we sell them:** not another queue — a dossier
attached to the candidate they were already about to submit, and a reason to
submit them *faster* because the evidence is already assembled.

### Compliance trigger — the client's vendor management office
Doesn't buy from us, but writes the requirement that makes us necessary. The
verification clause in the MSA is our best salesperson.

### Beneficiary — the candidate
Doesn't pay. Gets the verification badge and the "is this recruiter real?" check
for free, because the agency is paying to protect its own brand.

## 4. Pricing thesis

Agencies think in placements, not seats. So we price in submissions.

**Per verified submission**, with a monthly floor — not per-recruiter licensing.
It matches their unit economics exactly: the cost is incurred only when revenue
is being pursued, and it scales down in a slow quarter instead of becoming dead
overhead. A recruiter will spend a few dollars per submission to avoid the one
that costs them a $2M logo.

Seat-based pricing would fail here, and it's a large part of why the incumbents
don't reach this segment.

## 5. Beachhead and expansion

1. **IT / contract staffing** (now) — highest fraud rate, fully remote, sanctions
   exposure, most acute client-audit pressure.
2. **Healthcare and allied staffing** — credential fraud carries patient-safety
   consequence, and state licensure registries are *structured public data*,
   which makes corroboration dramatically stronger than in tech.
3. **MSPs and RPOs** — they aggregate dozens of agencies and can mandate
   Groundtruth across their supplier panel. Channel, not direct sales.
4. **SMB direct employers** — same product, later, once the trust badge has
   circulation among candidates.

## 6. Where we deliberately do not play

- **Enterprise TA suites.** Twelve-month procurement, entrenched incumbents,
  partial coverage already. We would win the bake-off and lose the year.
- **Consumer anti-scam apps.** The people who need it most cannot pay, and
  charging them is the wrong business. We serve them through the agency instead.
- **Identity verification / liveness.** Persona, Clear and others do this well.
  We integrate; we don't rebuild. Our surface is the *application*, upstream of
  any interview.
- **Interview proctoring.** Downstream of us. Complementary, not competitive.

## 7. Honest competitive read

This is not a green field. Candidate-fraud point solutions exist and are
emerging quickly. But they cluster at **interview time** — proctoring, liveness,
proxy detection — which fires after a recruiter has already invested hours, and
they price and sell to enterprises.

Two gaps remain open, and they are the ones we take:

1. **Application time, at agency price points.** Nothing meaningful sits at the
   moment of submission, affordable to a 30-person firm, that produces an
   auditable artifact.
2. **Both directions.** Every competitor secures one side of the funnel. None
   turns the same verification engine around to let a candidate check the
   recruiter. For staffing specifically — the most impersonated party in hiring
   — that second direction is not a feature. It is brand protection for the
   customer's core business motion.
